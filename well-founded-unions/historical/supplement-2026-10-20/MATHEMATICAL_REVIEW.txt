FOUR RELATIONS — INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL REVIEW AND PROOF MAP
20 October 2026

Scope and correspondence
------------------------
Every line of the 1,280-line input termination development and the standalone
counterexample was read, together with both the supplied AMS manuscript and
the newer LMCS finite manuscript. Proof scripts were checked against their
definitions, not inferred from theorem names. The final termination source
differs in its introductory status comment only from the latest locally found
proof-repaired version; all additional results are in Refinements.v.

The typed audit expands the finite theorem's unions, composition direction,
well-foundedness convention, and every alternative of P0/P1/Q2 independently.
Its only shared closure is the inspected inductive finite-path relation star.
The other typed aliases check the finite-image, restricted uniform-height,
pointwise, branching, core, and local-branching statements. Complete printed
types and assumptions are retained, including the abstract rank hypotheses.

General Jumping, Tripartite, Detour, and the positive four-relation theorem
are cited published results, not claimed as newly Rocq-checked in their full
generality. The specialized detour and binary-union results used by the new
proofs are proved internally. The historical first surviving-tail assertion,
finite search normal forms, surjective exact-size reduction, and encoder
correctness are retained in the archived source, not the revised manuscript.
These are mathematical/computational arguments, not new Rocq claims. The original
published positive result, including rearrangement, remains attributed to
Dawson, Dershowitz, and Goré.

Dependency structure and independent checks
-------------------------------------------
1. Accessibility is used for forward edges by reversing Rocq's relation.
   The positive-length closure has at least one edge. Finiteness is a covering
   list, not an assumed decidable equality or enumerable universe.
   Nonaccessibility propagates backwards along finite paths. Consequently
   every rerouting witness ending at an immortal point remains immortal.

2. The serial_rerouting_impossible proof first minimizes under P, then under Q,
   and minimizes a set of Q-successors under P. The last minimization supplies
   a P-minimal Q-successor without choosing an infinite sequence. This proves
   the required weak jumping and detour instances. They are not postulates.

3. A-normalization provides existential representatives of all immortal
   targets. P0 preserves incoming E-edges from A-normal sources. A nonempty
   representative core therefore has a serial E-restriction. With no active
   B-target, its F-edges survive the proved detour constriction, contradiction.

4. The folding proof chooses a PREDECESSOR-free active target for B+, not a
   forward-normal target. This direction is essential. A two-or-more-edge
   B-path from the core to it is impossible. P1 therefore transfers incoming
   E-edges from all deleted B-predecessors to a surviving representative.
   That representative survives because otherwise irreflexivity of E fails.
   The finite-list induction deletes a target, even with repeated list entries.
   It changes neither relations nor theorem hypotheses.

5. The bounded-height proof removes a whole maximum-height layer. Incoming
   domination is transitive; between points of that layer it cannot contain
   a B-edge. Its intersections with C and D are well-founded, so the proved
   transitive-subunion result applies. Accessibility yields a finite domination
   path to the lower layer. Transferring incoming edges along that path
   preserves representatives. Natural-number induction supplies the final
   contradiction; no transfinite induction is silently used here.

6. Under strong P1, F-edges out of A-minimal points propagate every nonempty
   B-path without shortening it. Thus a fixed finite-height slice of I_A is
   closed under E-edges between minimal points. For a target with an incoming
   edge from this slice, its initial representative is in the slice. With no
   such incoming edge, a fixed seed represents it vacuously. This gives a
   bounded core and proves the stronger I_A conclusion.

7. Local branching restricts B to the B*-reachable cone of the selected
   minimal immortal point. Every path from that point stays in the cone,
   and accessibility is inherited by restriction. Finite branching there
   gives a bound by accessibility induction and finite-list maxima, without
   dependent choice. The new formal result proves the negated finite-cover
   statement at some reachable point, not merely the failure of a global bound.

8. The rank proof uses only a strict total order, strict decrease on B, and
   the displayed rank-bound law. At the least attained rank, its level set
   is a core. An empty lower segment gives no B-target. A greatest lower value
   beta must be attained by a B-successor: otherwise the bound law lowers
   the source rank. Reroutings to rank beta have one B-edge because no
   intermediate rank fits. This makes domination serial and well-founded,
   a contradiction. Ordinal ranks satisfy these laws by their usual supremum
   definition, but their set-theoretic construction is outside the Rocq scope.

9. The concrete example was checked directly: A and C have no two-step path,
   D has no three-step path, and B after leaving p or q decreases the selected
   natural number. The seven displayed P0'/P1' reroutings and the single Q2'
   detour have exactly the stated endpoints. The union has the explicit
   p,q,p,q,... chain. Every vertex is immortal; I_A={p,q}; the escape targets
   are precisely N; height(n)=n; rank(p)=rank(q)=omega.
   Therefore countability of targets and pointwise finite height at the
   targets themselves do not suffice, even under the stronger middle premise.

10. The historical surviving-tail proof iterates P0 and P1 by finite path
    induction, then minimizes within the sets of admissible successors.
    Appending the remainder of the path preserves an AR* alternative.
    For search normalization, a sink component of the immortal restriction
    contains a cycle and is closed under reroutings. A shortest F-cycle has
    no extra directed chord. Its monochromaticity would contradict C or D
    acyclicity. The L=2 detour follows from Q2, and its B-target is outside I_A.
    Surjective lifting preserves acyclicity and positive-length reroutings;
    a cyclic strongly connected component also has positive-length paths
    from each vertex to itself, as needed when two preimages share an image.
    These checks support the historical reductions independently of the new
    finite theorem, which would otherwise make their premises vacuous.

Research limitations
--------------------
The original shortcut option can increase B-height across an F-edge, so the
pointwise proof cannot be transplanted unchanged to P1. No proof or counterexample
settling that broader question was found here. The manuscript says so explicitly.
No claim of a complete classification, or of new solver proof certificates,
is made. A finite or bounded core is a sufficient condition, not an assertion
that every infinite subset of I_A has the representative property.
